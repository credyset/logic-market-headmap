# Logic Agency Market Language Map

**Source:** 6,854 classified podcast transcript chunks across 332 Startup CPG episodes, 17 agency-relevant topics.
**Date:** 2026-09-01
**Evidence rule:** Every term, phrase, or pattern cited below is drawn from the discovered topic taxonomy, topic distribution, co-occurrence data, trend analysis, or extracted claims CSV. No quotes are fabricated. Paraphrases are labeled.

---

## 1. Terms buyers actually use

Organized by parent theme, these are the natural-language phrases and concepts that appear repeatedly across the corpus when founders and operators discuss topics relevant to Logic Agency's services.

### Retail and distribution operations (60.3% of all agency mentions)

**Getting into retail**
- "retail buyer readiness" -- the single most frequent topic cluster (3,685 mentions, 14.06% share)
- "category fit" / "what even is your category" -- how buyers frame the gatekeeping question (Michael Brandt, #229)
- "category review calendars" -- the operational constraint founders don't anticipate
- "get on the shelf" / "stay on the shelf" -- the canonical two-part challenge (Veena Krishnan: "It's easy to get on the shelf, it's hard to stay on the shelf")
- "retail velocity" -- the metric that determines shelf survival; co-occurs heavily with in-store demos (1,944 co-occurrences between demos and buyer readiness)
- "EDI compliant" -- a specific readiness gate that catches brands off guard

**Growing wholesale**
- "wholesale expansion" / "buyer relationships" -- second-highest topic (3,101 mentions, 11.83%)
- "promotional calendars" / "investing on their marketing" -- the hidden cost of retail (Maria Covarrubia: "extremely expensive because of the expectation of having promotional calendars")
- "checkout" / "how competitive and expensive checkout is" -- shelf placement economics
- "skew fest" -- pejorative for over-assorted retail environments (Cam Mathias on Petco)
- "bottoms up forecast" -- the forecasting methodology founders describe when talking to brokers and distributors

**Demos and velocity**
- "in-store demos" / "promotions" -- third-highest topic (2,575 mentions, 9.82%)
- "get a promotion to actually happen" -- the small-brand struggle (Daniel Scharff: "Obviously hard to do that as a small brand")
- "giving people a free product at the retail price, not just your cost" -- the economics of sampling

**Brokers and distribution**
- "broker" -- the most discussed intermediary in the corpus (1,548 mentions for broker selection)
- "find the right broker" / "talk to a lot of them" -- the search process
- "broker for natural, a broker for convenience, a broker for conventional" -- channel-specific broker language
- "extension of my business" -- aspirational framing for broker relationship
- "paying brokers a ton of money to get on shelves" -- the cost complaint
- "distributor" / "DSD" -- distinct channel execution (866 mentions)
- "chicken or the egg" -- the distributor-retailer sequencing problem (Grace Kennedy)

**Regional expansion**
- "local-to-national" -- the growth path language used in the corpus (1,957 mentions)
- "cold chain logistics" / "shipping temperature control" -- the physical expansion constraint
- "deliver across the country" / "very expensive" -- freight cost awareness

### Manufacturing and supplier management (16.0% of all mentions)

**Contracts and risk**

- "create problems if they know the company's about to sell" -- contract leverage risk (Ryan Hall)

### Financial and operational control (8.1% of all mentions)

## 3. Terms to avoid or reframe

Language that does not match how buyers talk, based on its absence from or misalignment with the corpus.

| Avoid | Why | Reframe as |
|---|---|---|
| "Fractional COO" / "fractional operations" | Not found in buyer vocabulary. Founders think in terms of problems, not org chart titles. | "Your ops team" / "the ops team you'd hire but can't yet afford" |
| "End-to-end supply chain management" | Too abstract. Founders describe specific breakdowns, not holistic systems. | Name the specific pain: "co-man selection," "demand planning," "3PL management" |
| "Strategic advisory" | Triggers the "consultant who doesn't execute" objection. Katherine Harvey's "outsourced operations outfit just brokering" quote reveals the allergy. | "We execute" / "we do the work" -- emphasize hands-on delivery |
| "Optimization" (standalone) | Overused and vague. Founders say "unit economics," "gross margins," "contribution margins" -- specific financial language. | Use the specific metric: "improve your gross margin," "fix your landed cost" |
| "Operational excellence" | Corporate jargon. Zero occurrences in founder-spoken corpus segments. | "Getting ops right" / "ops that don't keep you up at night" |
| "Value-added services" | Sales-speak. Founders never describe what they buy this way. | Name the service: "packaging engineering," "3PL negotiation," "demand forecasting" |
| "Scalable infrastructure" | VC-pitch language, not operator language. Founders say "scale up" when they mean production, not infrastructure. | "Ready to scale production" / "ready for your next 100K units" |
| "Digital transformation" | Not relevant to the CPG ops audience. AI is discussed (403 mentions) but never framed as "transformation." | "Using AI for [specific task]" -- recipe costing, forecasting, SOP creation |
| "Holistic approach" | Consultant cliche absent from the corpus. | Describe the specific integration: "your co-man, your 3PL, and your forecasting, managed together" |
| "Synergies" | Zero founder usage. | Do not use. |

## 4. Pain language patterns

How founders describe operational pain, based on 587 extracted pain sentences and topic-level claim analysis.

### Pattern 1: The "nightmare" frame
Founders describe operational breakdowns using catastrophe language. The word "nightmare" appears across multiple speakers and episodes (Teva Vogelstein: "brands come to us because of ops nightmares"; Jamie Valenti-Jordan: "nightmares about ops that wake me up"). This is not hyperbole -- it signals that ops failures feel existential to founders.

**Implication for Logic:** The word "nightmare" is a legitimate positioning term. Content and ads can use it without exaggeration because founders already do.

### Pattern 2: The "burned" / "learned the hard way" frame
Founders describe past failures with betrayal language: "we got burned from that pretty bad" (Zoe Levin on packaging), "outsourced operations outfit just brokering this deal for us" (Katherine Harvey on co-man intermediaries). The pattern is: trusted a partner, got a bad outcome, now skeptical of all partners.

**Implication for Logic:** Logic's positioning must address the trust deficit. "We execute, we don't broker" directly counters Katherine Harvey's complaint. Case studies with named outcomes are essential to overcome the "burned before" frame.

### Pattern 3: The "too small / not ready yet" frame
Founders self-select out of getting help: "it's very hard to be small in the mass market area" (Jamie Valenti-Jordan), "there is a stage where companies are not yet working with us" (Peggy Shell, Creative Alignments). The pattern is: founders believe ops help is for companies bigger than theirs.

**Implication for Logic:** Logic's $2.5K/mo entry tier directly addresses this, but the corpus shows buyers don't know affordable ops support exists. Content should normalize early-stage ops investment with specific revenue thresholds ("You're doing $2M and managing ops yourself -- here's what that costs you").

### Pattern 4: The "chicken or the egg" frame
Founders describe sequencing dilemmas: distributor wants a retailer, retailer wants a distributor (Grace Kennedy); need volume for co-man pricing, need pricing for volume. The pattern is: operational decisions are interdependent and founders feel stuck.

**Implication for Logic:** Logic can position as the entity that breaks the sequencing deadlock. "We've done this 50 times" is more compelling than "we'll help you figure it out."

### Pattern 5: The "didn't know what we didn't know" frame
Founders describe being blindsided by requirements they didn't anticipate: category review calendars (Lindsay Hancock), EDI compliance (Emily Curran), promotional calendar costs (Maria Covarrubia). The pattern is: the problem wasn't hard -- it was invisible until it was urgent.

**Implication for Logic:** Checklists and readiness assessments are high-value lead magnets because they surface the "didn't know" problems before they become crises. Logic's existing retail readiness guide serves this function.

## 5. Buyer vocabulary by journey stage

Based on topic trends and co-occurrence patterns, the language buyers use shifts as they grow. Early-stage brands (pre-retail or first retailer) use different vocabulary than scaling brands (regional to national) or mature brands (optimizing existing operations).

### Early stage (pre-retail to first retailer)

**Primary topics:** Retail buyer readiness (3,685 mentions), Product development (1,223 mentions), Co-manufacturer selection (1,873 mentions)

**Vocabulary:**
- "How do I get on the shelf" / "what do buyers want"
- "Finding a co-man" / "our formulation is really difficult to make"
- "Category fit" / "what even is your category"
- "EDI" / "what is EDI" / "EDI compliant"
- "We're doing everything ourselves" / "painful stuff that's administrative"
- "Pricing" / "what should my margins be"
- "Packaging" / "labeling requirements" / "certifications"

**Emotional register:** Overwhelmed, uncertain, asking permission-type questions. High guest-voice share (69.9% for product development, 73.3% for packaging -- these are founder-dominated conversations).

### Scaling stage (regional to national, multiple retailers)

**Primary topics:** Wholesale expansion (3,101 mentions), In-store demos (2,575 mentions), Regional expansion (1,957 mentions), Broker selection (1,548 mentions), Inventory planning (973 mentions)

**Vocabulary:**
- "Retail velocity" / "staying on the shelf" / "getting it off the shelf"
- "Broker" / "find the right broker" / "paying brokers a ton of money"
- "Local to national" / "cold chain logistics"
- "Promotional calendars" / "promotions are expensive"
- "Bottoms up forecast" / "demand forecasting"
- "Working capital problems" / "cash flow"
- "Distributor" / "DSD" / "chicken or the egg"

**Emotional register:** Frustrated, cost-conscious, dealing with complexity they didn't anticipate. More action-oriented language -- "how do I" becomes "how do I do this better."

### Mature stage (optimizing, multi-channel)

**Primary topics:** Gross margin and unit economics (956 mentions, rising +253%), Amazon/DTC channel economics (2,091 mentions), Co-man contracts and risk (1,104 mentions, rising +179%), AI-enabled ops (403 mentions, rising +220%)

**Vocabulary:**
- "Unit economics" / "gross margins" / "contribution margins"
- "Channel strategy" / "DTC to retail" / "Amazon economics"
- "Co-manufacturer contracts" / "IP" / "liability"
- "Scale-up" / "process control"
- "AI" / "automation" / "EDI automation"
- "Chargebacks" / "compliance" / "routing guides"

**Emotional register:** Analytical, metric-driven, focused on efficiency and risk management. Language shifts from "how do I" to "how do I optimize" and "how do I protect." The +253% rise in gross margin language and +179% rise in contract language suggest this mature-stage vocabulary is growing rapidly in the market.

## Methodology note

Topic labels and co-occurrence data come from unsupervised clustering of 6,854 transcript chunks. Pain language patterns are extracted from 587 claim sentences tagged as pain_language in the corpus. Role analysis (guest vs. host share) indicates whether language originates from founders/operators (guests) or the podcast host. Trend directions compare the second half of the data chronologically against the first half. No Logic first-party content appears in the corpus.
