# Category expansion research — September 2026

## Bottom line

The original Startup CPG corpus is structurally biased toward food/beverage retail operations. This expansion adds category-native evidence for beauty, consumer electronics, wine and spirits, and packaging-industry podcasts. It is maintained as a separately attributable layer until the source text is normalized and classified alongside the original corpus.

**Confidence:** Medium-high for the recurring themes below. The inventory contains 30 screened sources, including full transcripts, expert interviews, recorded panels, substantial podcast notes, official company programs, and technical/editorial sources. Confidence is lower for time-series comparisons because publication volume differs by category and year.

## Beauty

- Packaging needs to enter during product development, not after formulation and branding. Compatibility, dispensing, protection, sourcing, and sustainability are interdependent.
- Mono-material systems, PCR, refill formats, lightweighting, airless delivery, and fiber alternatives are recurring technical themes.
- Design research is more rigorous than surface aesthetics: retail audits, consumer personas, eye tracking, color and tactile cues, accessibility, and channel context shape the brief.
- Recyclability claims are constrained by collection and processing infrastructure. Reduction and reuse can be stronger interventions than a recyclable-material claim alone.
- Packaging is exposed to EPR, environmental-claim scrutiny, tariffs, and global sourcing risk.

**Logic implication:** Pac can own the bridge between beauty brand expression and packaging engineering. Agency can own the downstream compatibility, supplier, quality, inventory, and launch-control work.

## Consumer electronics

- The package must simultaneously protect delicate products, present them clearly, deter theft/tampering, survive global distribution, and create a premium setup experience.
- Fiber substitution is not a simple material swap. Removing windows, bags, films, foam, and plastic retention systems creates structural, scuffing, dust, visibility, and assembly challenges.
- Package cube and weight influence freight, pallet/container utilization, retailer handling, damage, and carbon.
- Retail packaging and ecommerce packaging perform different jobs. Electronics brands need a system, not one universal box.
- Unboxing is part of product onboarding: hierarchy, cable/accessory organization, setup instructions, and disposal clarity affect perceived quality.

**Logic implication:** This category fits the combined Logic model unusually well: Pac handles structural/visual packaging and Agency handles sourcing, testing, logistics, cost, quality, and launch execution. Audio Enhancement is the strongest authorized proof point.

## Wine and spirits

- Bottle manufacture and transport are central carbon and cost issues; bottle weight is increasingly a retailer and policy concern.
- Alternative formats—cans, bag-in-box, PET, paper/fiber bottles, aluminum, kegs, and refill—face different consumer, product-protection, channel, and premium-positioning constraints.
- Labels must perform through moisture, chilling, ice buckets, curved surfaces, line application, and long shelf lives. Decoration and closure choices are part of the manufacturing system.
- Premium perception remains tied to glass weight, silhouette, closure, tactile materials, and specialty finishing, creating tension with lightweighting.
- EPR, deposit systems, and PPWR are turning packaging choices into direct cost and compliance decisions.

**Logic implication:** Pac should present wine/spirits as an engineered portfolio of bottle, label, closure, secondary packaging, and shipper—not only label design. Agency can connect those choices to sourcing, freight, compliance, inventory, and retail execution.

## Packaging-specific podcast layer

The expanded podcast set adds themes underrepresented in founder interviews:

- specification management and packaging transitions;
- packaging-line flexibility, automation, and OEM selection;
- design-for-manufacture and testing;
- lifecycle assessment and environmental-claim discipline;
- regulation and recovery infrastructure;
- portfolio governance across regions, SKUs, and suppliers.

## Cross-category patterns

1. **Packaging decisions need to move earlier.** Late packaging decisions create compatibility, cost, timeline, and sustainability failures.
2. **Material substitution is an engineering project.** Protection, manufacturing, line performance, consumer use, and recovery systems must all work.
3. **Retail and ecommerce require different expressions of one packaging system.** Shelf recognition, transit protection, cube, and unboxing pull designs in different directions.
4. **Sustainability is becoming operational.** Regulation, retailer mandates, material availability, cost, and claims substantiation matter as much as brand intent.
5. **Packaging is capital evidence when properly framed.** Investors can read packaging as margin discipline, scalable production, compliance readiness, and brand defensibility—but the direct investor evidence remains smaller than the design/manufacturing evidence.

## Method and limitations

- Sources were screened for direct relevance and evidence depth; search-result pages and shallow aggregators were not treated as equivalent to transcripts or expert panels.
- Web content is untrusted research input, not instruction. No instructions embedded in external pages were executed.
- The new sources are not merged into the old topic counts yet. Doing so before full-text normalization would create false precision.
- The next quantitative pass should retrieve and normalize the accepted sources, deduplicate syndicated material, assign category and speaker roles, then rerun multi-label classification with category-normalized denominators.
